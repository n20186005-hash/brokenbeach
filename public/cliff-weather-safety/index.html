Staying safe at the cliff edge

Broken Beach is an open cliff-top site above the sea. The main risks are unguarded edges, large swell, wind and weathered limestone, not facilities or crowds.

Edge and wave risk

Waves can reach the cliff base even when the surface looks calm. Stay behind barriers and marked edges, keep children close, and never turn your back on the sea while near the lip.

Weather and footing

Limestone becomes slippery when wet, and afternoon wind can be strong on the headland. Wear good footwear, avoid the edge when it rains or the sea is rough, and turn back if conditions worsen.

When to skip or leave

If a storm, high swell or poor visibility arrives, leave the cliff area. Regional model data on this site is not a safety guarantee and should never replace local judgement.

  • Do not pass barriers for a photo.
  • Keep a safe distance from the cliff lip at all times.
  • Watch children and less mobile companions constantly.

QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

Is Broken Beach dangerous?

The site is safe to view from established areas, but the cliff edge is unguarded and the sea can be large. Follow barriers and local guidance.

What if the weather turns bad?

Leave the cliff area if storms, high swell or poor visibility arrive. Do not rely on model data for safety decisions.

Can children visit?

Yes, with close supervision. Keep them away from the unguarded edge and hold hands near the viewing rim.
