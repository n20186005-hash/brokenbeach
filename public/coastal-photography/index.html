Photographing the arch without the risk

Broken Beach's circular opening frames the sea like a window, which is why it shows up in so many Nusa Penida photos. The best shots come from the established viewing areas, not from climbing closer to the edge.

Where to point the camera

The main viewing spot looks down into the cove through the rock arch, with the open sea behind it. From there you can also capture the green cliff ring and the small beach inside the circle.

Light and timing

Mid-morning often gives clearer, less hazy light on the west coast, while late afternoon warms the cliff face. There is no safe swimming or descent, so plan photos from the permitted viewing level rather than the water.

Safety and etiquette

Stay behind barriers and marked edges. Do not fly a drone without local permission, and never step past a fence for a wider angle. If the sea is high, keep more distance from the cliff lip.

  • Use a zoom lens instead of moving closer to the edge.
  • Watch footing on uneven, weathered limestone.
  • Avoid blocking the path for other visitors.

QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

What is the best time of day to photograph Broken Beach?

Mid-morning usually offers clearer light on this west-coast side, with late afternoon adding warm colour to the cliff. Conditions vary, so check the sky on the day.

Can I use a drone here?

Only with local permission. Assume drone flight is restricted unless you have confirmed otherwise, and follow Indonesian rules.

Is there a safe place to stand for the classic arch shot?

Yes, from the established viewing area above the cove. Do not descend toward the water or climb unprotected edges for a photo.
